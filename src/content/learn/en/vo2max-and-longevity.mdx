Most of the numbers on a health tracker tell you how you are right now: your pulse, your sleep last night, your blood pressure this morning. VO₂max is different. It is a measure of capacity — how much work your body can do before it runs out of oxygen — and it turns out to be one of the strongest single predictors of how long you will live. It is also, unusually, a number you can move.

## What VO₂max actually measures

![](/learn-img-2/vo2max-and-longevity.jpg)

VO₂max is the maximum rate at which your body can take in and use oxygen during hard, escalating exercise. It is written in millilitres of oxygen per kilogram of body weight per minute (mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹), and it captures the whole oxygen-delivery chain working at full stretch: your lungs pulling air in, your heart pumping blood, your blood carrying oxygen, and your muscles burning it. The fitter that chain, the higher the number, and the more comfortably you can climb stairs, carry shopping, or hold a conversation while walking uphill.

In a laboratory it is measured directly, with a mask that samples the air you breathe while you exercise to exhaustion. That gas-exchange test is the gold standard and the reason VO₂max is treated as the best single index of aerobic fitness.[^aha2016]

## Why your wearable is estimating, not measuring

The device on your wrist has no mask and no way to sample your breath. What it has is your heart rate and your pace — and a model that connects them. The logic is that, for a given person, a faster pace at a lower heart rate signals higher fitness, so the algorithm watches that relationship during runs or walks and infers a VO₂max from it.

That inference is genuinely useful, but it is an estimate, not a measurement, and several things blur it. The model leans on an assumed maximum heart rate, which is itself only a population guess. Optical wrist sensors lose accuracy during motion — exactly when this number is being computed. And the figure shifts with terrain, heat, and how the device fits.[^acsm] The honest way to read a wearable VO₂max is as a trend line: the absolute value may be a few points off, but a steady climb over weeks is real, and a steady decline is worth noticing.

<Aside type="note">
Lab tests on a stationary bike typically read about 10–15% lower than treadmill tests, and a tested maximum heart rate beats the textbook estimate every time. Compare like with like — your own number over time, on the same kind of activity — rather than one figure against someone else's.
</Aside>

## The link to a longer life

This is where VO₂max earns its reputation. Cardiorespiratory fitness is an independent, graded predictor of all-cause and cardiovascular mortality — meaning that across large populations, fitter people die less, the relationship holds even after accounting for other risk factors, and it strengthens steadily as fitness rises. Each one-MET increment of fitness — roughly 3.5 mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹, about the gain from getting meaningfully more active — is associated with somewhere around a 10–25% lower risk of death.[^aha2016] The American Heart Association has gone as far as to argue fitness should be treated as a clinical vital sign, measured and acted on like blood pressure.[^aha2016]

The behaviour underneath the number has the same weight of evidence. Accumulated aerobic minutes combined with resistance training carry some of the strongest dose-response evidence in all of preventive medicine — for reduced cardiovascular disease, type-2 diabetes, several cancers, depression, and death from any cause.[^who2020][^hhs2018] The point is not that a wrist gadget extends your life. It is that the gadget tracks a capacity that genuinely tracks mortality.

## How to read your own number

VO₂max is interpreted as a **percentile within your own age and sex band**, never against a fixed target. Men test roughly 15–20% higher than women at the same age, and fitness falls with age, so a value that is excellent at 60 would be unremarkable at 25. The table below shows the median (50th-percentile) value by decade from the reference dataset behind the widely used fitness categories.[^friend2015]

| Age band | Men (mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹) | Women (mL·kg⁻¹·min⁻¹) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 20–29 | 48.0 | 37.6 |
| 30–39 | 42.4 | 30.2 |
| 40–49 | 37.8 | 26.7 |
| 50–59 | 32.6 | 23.4 |
| 60–69 | 28.2 | 20.0 |
| 70–79 | 24.4 | 18.3 |

Within each band, fitness is graded by percentile: below the 20th is poor, the 40th–59th is average, the 80th–94th is excellent, and the 95th or above is superior.[^acsm] The figures in the table are a yardstick, not a scoreboard — what matters more than where you sit today is which direction you are moving.

## How the number responds to training

The encouraging part is that VO₂max is one of the few mortality-linked numbers you can change. Aerobic fitness rises with consistent training and falls with detraining, age, illness, altitude, and anaemia. Untrained, it declines about 10% per decade after 30; regular aerobic training roughly halves that to about 5% per decade — effectively buying back years of capacity.[^friend2015]

Moving it is not complicated, but it is unglamorous. The weekly target, identical across the major guidelines, is 150–300 minutes of moderate activity or 75–150 minutes of vigorous activity, plus muscle-strengthening work at least twice a week. (The old rule that exercise had to come in ten-minute blocks has been dropped — any duration now counts.)[^who2020][^hhs2018] Intensity is relative to your own fitness: moderate is the pace where you can talk but not sing, vigorous is where you can manage only a few words at a time.[^acsm] On top of that, recovery is part of the work — about 24–48 hours between sessions that stress the same muscles, with increases kept small and gradual.[^acsm]

<Aside type="caution">
Before starting vigorous exercise — especially if you are over 40, have been inactive for a long time, or have any heart, lung, or metabolic condition — check with a clinician first. And if your fitness is falling alongside breathlessness, chest pain, or unexplained fatigue, that is a medical conversation, not a training tweak. Genuine overtraining is a *cluster* that holds for about a week — a persistently raised [resting heart rate](/learn/resting-heart-rate), suppressed [HRV](/learn/heart-rate-variability), poor sleep, declining performance, and low mood together — and it overlaps heavily with ordinary illness and life stress, so a single off day means little.[^meeusen2013]
</Aside>

## The bigger picture

VO₂max does not stand alone. It rises as your [resting heart rate](/learn/resting-heart-rate) falls, moves with your [heart rate variability](/learn/heart-rate-variability), and depends on the [sleep](/learn/sleep-consistency) and recovery that let training stick. Read on its own, a wearable's estimate is a rough gauge with real error bars. Read as a trend, alongside those other signals, it is a window onto the one form of fitness most consistently tied to living longer.

This is general information, not medical advice, and individual variation is wide. Anchor on your own baseline; use the population ranges as a sanity check, not a scoreboard.
